TriSphere model configurations

A. Beth Jones’s thirteen-layer model and alternate Five-Structure, compared under the same routing and boundary settings.

Shared controls

Each run starts with one unit of material and one unit of energy at the center. Both configurations use the same clock and retain the center and outer shell as separate boundaries.

How the comparison works

The primary model routes Product to layer 7 and Compost to layers 8–11, then deposits returning material at their mirror shelves. The Five-Structure groups layers 1–4 into the lower membrane, 5–7 into the avatar/Filter membrane, and 8–11 into the upper membrane. This grouping is a simulator choice. Layer 5 anchors the alternate avatar. Layer 6 is the Filter of the primary configuration.

The alternate keeps the transit time of compressed paths but pools shelf inventory by membrane. Its release rate is the arithmetic mean of the individual shelf rates in that membrane. This closure makes compression testable and is not derived from the blueprint. Both modes split the outbound flow at tick six and share the same outbound route clock. The upper membrane carries outbound flow and has no resting inventory under this routing rule. Containment blocks export at the shell, and damping transfers stored motion energy into an internal heat ledger. Exhaust can export separate fractions of material and energy. The powered drive adds a recorded amount at each completed return.

The model tracks discrete inventories and circulation times. It does not solve Jones’s continuous radial equation or establish limit-cycle stability. The Tokamak lab retains the separate center-kick example. A. Beth Jones’s TriSphere framework
